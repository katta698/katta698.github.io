How the venue warnings work, and what is a fact versus an estimate

The fact. Every session in the catalog carries its room — Caesars Palace | Promenade Level | Roman I — and a start and end time. When two sessions in your plan are at different properties, the gap between them is arithmetic on AWS's own published data. That number is what the warning states, and you can check it against the official catalog using the session code on every card.

The estimate. How long the hop takes is not published — AWS's FAQ says only to “allow for additional travel time between venues” and that shuttles “run continuously during conference hours”. So it is derived rather than guessed: the straight-line distance between the two properties, multiplied by 1.30 because a line between two hotels is not a route, divided by a walking pace of 60 m/min — slow on purpose, because this is casino floors and pedestrian bridges, not pavement — plus 10 min to get out of one room and into the next.

An earlier version used three flat numbers and was wrong in the direction that under-warns: it costed Wynn/Encore to Caesars Palace (1,443 m) at 30 minutes while costing Caesars Palace to MGM Grand (1,565 m) at 45. Distance is a fact; only the pace below is an assumption, and it is yours to set.

How current this is, and how you can tell. The catalog is re-fetched from AWS every day by a scheduled job, and the line at the top of this page works out its own age in your browser from the capture timestamp — so if the job ever stops running, the page says how old it is rather than quietly implying it is current. Three things guard the refresh: a fetch that comes back more than a fifth smaller than the stored catalog is refused rather than written, so a partial fetch cannot overwrite good data; the checks run before the commit, not after; and every session code on this page links to that session in AWS's own catalog, so nothing here has to be taken on trust. If a session you starred disappears from the catalog, your plan says so instead of quietly dropping it.
